1. PURPOSE

This policy sets out how the Company identifies, assesses, documents and manages the security risks and vulnerabilities affecting electronic protected health information (ePHI).

The Company conducts an accurate and thorough assessment of the potential risks and vulnerabilities to the confidentiality, integrity and availability of the ePHI it creates, receives, maintains or transmits.

What the assessment finds is used to put reasonable and appropriate security measures in place, to decide the order corrective actions are taken in, and to keep the Company within the HIPAA Security Rule.

2. SCOPE

This policy applies where the Company is a covered entity or a business associate subject to the Security Rule. It reaches:

  • Employees, contractors and other authorised workforce members.
  • Computer systems, applications and databases holding ePHI.
  • Mobile devices and driver applications that reach ePHI.
  • Dispatch, scheduling, billing and transportation management systems.
  • Remote access and work-from-home arrangements.
  • Electronic communications and data transmissions.
  • Cloud services, hosting providers and other third-party systems involving ePHI.

The Company records whether HIPAA reaches it, and looks at that question again when its business activities or its contracts materially change.

3. THE STANDING OBLIGATION

The Company maintains an ongoing risk analysis process designed to identify the threats and vulnerabilities to ePHI that are reasonably anticipated. It:

  1. Identifies every system and process involving ePHI.
  2. Evaluates the security threats and vulnerabilities to them.
  3. Reviews the administrative, physical and technical safeguards already in place.
  4. Assesses how likely each risk is and what it would cost.
  5. Records the findings and the level of each risk.
  6. Decides and carries out the corrective actions.
  7. Checks whether the measures taken actually work.
  8. Keeps the documentation the rule requires.
  9. Reassesses periodically, and whenever something significant changes.

Completing a checklist, or adopting this policy, does not by itself satisfy the risk analysis obligation. 45 CFR 164.308(a)(1)(ii)(A) asks for an assessment of this Company's own environment.

5. WHERE THE EPHI IS

The Company identifies where ePHI is created, received, maintained or transmitted. That includes, as applicable:

  • Passenger transportation records.
  • Passenger identification and contact information where it constitutes ePHI.
  • Appointment and trip information.
  • Medicaid, Medicare and other insurance information.
  • Billing and claims records.
  • Driver applications holding passenger information.
  • Electronic communications carrying ePHI.
  • Broker and payer integrations.
  • Backups and archived electronic records.

An inventory of the relevant systems, applications, devices and data locations is kept.

6. THREATS AND VULNERABILITIES

The Company identifies the threats and vulnerabilities that could reasonably be anticipated to compromise ePHI, among them:

  • Unauthorised access to passenger information.
  • Lost or stolen computers and mobile devices.
  • Weak passwords and compromised credentials.
  • Inadequate authentication or access control.
  • Malware, ransomware and phishing.
  • Improper transmission of passenger information.
  • Inadequate encryption in transit.
  • Insufficient security training.
  • Access left open to a former employee.
  • Disclosure through a third-party integration.
  • Inadequate backup and recovery.
  • Theft, fire, flood and equipment failure.

Each is evaluated against how this Company actually operates, not against a generic list.

7. THE SAFEGUARDS ALREADY IN PLACE

The Company evaluates the administrative, physical and technical safeguards protecting ePHI.

Administrative safeguards include workforce authorisation, security training, access management, incident response, contingency planning and vendor oversight.

Physical safeguards include facility access control, workstation protection, device management and secure disposal.

Technical safeguards include authentication, access control, audit logging, encryption, integrity protection and secure transmission.

For each, the assessment records whether it is in place and working, in place but insufficient, or absent.

8. HOW RISK IS RATED

Each risk is evaluated on a consistent basis that takes account of how likely the threat is to occur or to exploit the vulnerability, what it would do to the confidentiality, integrity or availability of ePHI, the safeguards already in place, and how severe the consequences would be.

Risks may be classified Low, Moderate, High or Critical. The method, the ratings and the reasoning behind them are recorded in the risk analysis.

No numerical scoring system is required unless the Company adopts one.

9. THE RISK RECORD

For each risk the Company records the system or process affected, the threat, the vulnerability, the safeguards already in place, the likelihood and the impact, the classification, the corrective action recommended, who is responsible for it, the date it is due, and where it has got to.

Records are updated as corrective actions are carried out and as new information arrives.

10. CORRECTIVE ACTION

The Company puts security measures in place sufficient to reduce the identified risks to a reasonable and appropriate level - 45 CFR 164.308(a)(1)(ii)(B).

Corrective actions are taken in the order the severity of the risks calls for. Each is assigned to somebody with a date to do it by, and completion is recorded and, where it matters, verified.

Management accepting a risk does not authorise non-compliance with the Security Rule.

11. THIS BUSINESS IN PARTICULAR

Driver devices. Unauthorised access, loss and theft, passenger information held on the device, how the driver authenticates, and how access ends when they leave.

Dispatch and scheduling. Who can see passenger identities, pickup and destination addresses, appointment information and the rest of it.

Broker and payer integrations. The electronic exchange of passenger and trip information - interfaces, file transfers and any other route data takes.

Billing and claims. Access to passenger identifiers, payer information, claims records and the related transactions.

Remote workers. Remote access, personal devices, authentication, endpoint protection and the handling of ePHI away from Company premises.

Vendors. The security risks arising from anybody who creates, receives, maintains or transmits ePHI for the Company. What the business associate agreements require is dealt with separately.

12. HOW OFTEN

The Company conducts a risk analysis at the outset and periodically after that, on a schedule its environment, its risks and its changes justify. It reassesses when:

  • A new system or application is introduced.
  • An existing system changes significantly.
  • A new broker or payer integration goes in.
  • Remote access arrangements materially change.
  • An incident or breach reveals a vulnerability.
  • A new threat or vulnerability is identified.
  • The Company's operations or its handling of ePHI materially change.

13. INCIDENTS

A security incident affecting ePHI is examined for whether it reveals a risk nobody had identified or calls the existing safeguards into question. Findings are carried across to the Company's incident response procedures, and a vulnerability it exposes is added to the risk record with a corrective action against it.

This policy does not replace the breach notification requirements.

14. WHAT IS KEPT

The Company keeps the completed risk analyses, the system and ePHI inventories, the method used, the risks and vulnerabilities identified, the risk management decisions, the corrective action plans, the supporting evidence, the reviews and updates, and the management approvals.

Documentation required under the Security Rule is retained for at least six years from the date it was created or the date it was last in effect, whichever is later - 45 CFR 164.316(b)(2)(i). It is protected against unauthorised access, alteration and destruction.

15. MANAGEMENT REVIEW

The Security Official reports the significant risks and the order corrective action should be taken in to Company management, which reviews the findings and sees that the resources and responsibilities needed are assigned.

Approving a risk analysis is not a statement that every security risk has been eliminated.

16. REVIEW OF THIS POLICY

This policy is reviewed periodically and revised when the regulatory requirements, the Company's operations or its security practices materially change. Changes are recorded and approved.

17. COMPLIANCE

Employees and authorised personnel cooperate with the Company's risk analysis activities and follow its security policies. Failing to do so may lead to corrective or disciplinary action, consistent with Company policy and the law.
