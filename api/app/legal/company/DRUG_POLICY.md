## 1. PURPOSE

{company} is committed to protecting the safety of its passengers, employees, drivers, and the public.

The purpose of this policy is to establish a controlled substances and alcohol testing program that complies with the requirements of the U.S. Department of Transportation (DOT), Federal Motor Carrier Safety Administration (FMCSA), including 49 CFR Parts 40 and 382.

Compliance with this policy and participation in the required DOT drug and alcohol testing program are conditions of performing DOT-regulated safety-sensitive functions for {company}.

Nothing in this policy is intended to reduce or eliminate any requirement imposed by federal or state law.

---

## 2. DRIVERS COVERED BY THIS POLICY

This DOT policy applies to every employee, applicant, temporary employee, part-time employee, intermittent driver, backup driver, contractor, owner, or other individual who performs a safety-sensitive function for {company} and is subject to the CDL requirements of 49 CFR Part 383.

Generally, this includes a driver who operates a commercial motor vehicle for which a Commercial Driver's License (CDL) or Commercial Learner's Permit (CLP) is required.

A driver is subject to this policy based on the safety-sensitive function performed and the vehicle being operated, not merely the driver's job title.

Drivers who operate only vehicles that do not require a CDL are not subject to DOT testing under Part 382 solely because they work for an NEMT company or because they possess a CDL.

{company} may maintain a separate non-DOT drug and alcohol policy for employees who are not subject to Part 382. Any non-DOT testing will be conducted separately from DOT testing.

## 3. SAFETY-SENSITIVE FUNCTIONS

For purposes of this policy, safety-sensitive functions include all time from the time a driver begins work or is required to be ready to work until the driver is relieved from work and all responsibility for performing work.

Safety-sensitive functions include, as applicable:

- Waiting to be dispatched;
- Inspecting, servicing, or conditioning a commercial motor vehicle;
- Operating a commercial motor vehicle;
- Being in or upon a commercial motor vehicle, except while resting in a sleeper berth;
- Loading or unloading a vehicle, assisting or supervising loading or unloading, or attending a vehicle being loaded or unloaded;
- Remaining in readiness to operate a commercial motor vehicle;
- Performing duties associated with an accident; and
- Repairing, obtaining assistance for, or remaining in attendance upon a disabled commercial motor vehicle.

A covered driver must comply with DOT drug and alcohol requirements whenever performing or preparing to perform a safety-sensitive function.

## 5. PROHIBITED DRUG AND ALCOHOL CONDUCT

A covered driver shall not:

### A. Alcohol Concentration

Report for duty or remain on duty requiring the performance of a safety-sensitive function while having an alcohol concentration of **0.04 or greater**.

### B. Alcohol Use While Performing Safety-Sensitive Functions

Use alcohol while performing a safety-sensitive function.

### C. Pre-Duty Alcohol Use

Perform a safety-sensitive function within **four (4) hours after using alcohol**.

### D. Post-Accident Alcohol Use

When a driver is required to undergo post-accident alcohol testing, the driver shall not use alcohol for **eight (8) hours following the accident or until the driver undergoes the required post-accident alcohol test, whichever occurs first.**

### E. Controlled Substance Use

Report for duty or remain on duty requiring the performance of safety-sensitive functions when the driver uses a controlled substance prohibited by DOT regulations, except as permitted by applicable federal regulations for authorized medical use.

A driver using a prescription or other substance that may impair the driver's ability to safely operate a CMV is responsible for complying with applicable medical qualification and safety requirements.

### F. Positive Drug Test

Perform a safety-sensitive function after receiving a verified positive DOT drug test unless and until the driver has successfully completed the DOT return-to-duty process and is legally eligible to resume safety-sensitive functions.

### G. Refusal to Test

Refuse to submit to any DOT-required controlled substances or alcohol test.

A refusal to test is treated as a DOT drug and alcohol program violation.

## 7. REQUIRED TYPES OF TESTING

Covered drivers may be subject to the following testing:

### A. Pre-Employment Drug Testing

Before a driver is permitted to perform a DOT-regulated safety-sensitive function for {company}, the Company must receive a verified negative DOT drug test result unless a specific regulatory exception applies.

Pre-employment alcohol testing is not ordinarily required by FMCSA. If the Company elects to conduct DOT pre-employment alcohol testing, it will do so only in accordance with applicable federal regulations.

No covered driver may begin performing safety-sensitive functions until all applicable pre-employment DOT drug testing requirements have been satisfied.

### B. Random Testing

Covered drivers shall be included in an appropriate DOT random testing pool.

Random selections will be made using a scientifically valid method under which each covered driver has an equal chance of being selected each time selections are made.

Testing will be conducted at or above the minimum annual testing rates established by FMCSA.

Random tests will be unannounced and reasonably spread throughout the calendar year.

When notified of selection for a random test, the driver must proceed immediately to the testing location as directed.

Random alcohol testing will occur only while the driver is performing a safety-sensitive function, immediately before performing such a function, or immediately after performing such a function.

### C. Post-Accident Testing

Following an accident involving a CMV, {company} will determine whether DOT post-accident testing is required under 49 CFR 382.303.

DOT post-accident testing is required when regulatory criteria are met, including:

**Fatality:** Testing is required when the accident involves the loss of human life, regardless of whether the CMV driver receives a citation.

**Bodily Injury:** Testing is required when a person receives immediate medical treatment away from the accident scene AND the CMV driver receives a citation for a moving traffic violation arising from the accident.

**Disabling Damage:** Testing is required when one or more motor vehicles incur disabling damage requiring transportation away from the scene by a tow truck or other vehicle AND the CMV driver receives a citation for a moving traffic violation arising from the accident.

A required alcohol test should be administered as soon as practicable. If the test is not administered within two hours, the Company shall document the reasons for the delay. Attempts to obtain an alcohol test shall cease after eight hours.

A required controlled substances test shall be administered as soon as practicable. Attempts to obtain the controlled substances test shall cease after 32 hours.

A driver subject to post-accident testing must remain readily available for testing.

Nothing in this policy prohibits a driver from obtaining necessary emergency medical treatment.

### D. Reasonable Suspicion Testing

A driver shall submit to controlled substances and/or alcohol testing when {company} has reasonable suspicion, based upon specific, contemporaneous, articulable observations concerning the driver's appearance, behavior, speech, body odors, or other indicators recognized by applicable regulations.

The determination requiring reasonable suspicion testing will be made by a supervisor or Company official who has received the training required by 49 CFR 382.603.

Reasonable suspicion alcohol testing must comply with the applicable timing requirements for alcohol testing.

### E. Return-to-Duty Testing

A driver who has committed a DOT drug or alcohol violation shall not perform any DOT-regulated safety-sensitive function until the driver has successfully completed the return-to-duty process required by 49 CFR Part 40, Subpart O.

This process includes evaluation by a qualified Substance Abuse Professional ("SAP"), compliance with the SAP's education and/or treatment recommendations, and completion of a return-to-duty test with the result required by DOT regulations.

Return-to-duty testing is conducted under direct observation as required by DOT regulations.

Completion of the federal return-to-duty process does not guarantee employment or reinstatement with {company}.

### F. Follow-Up Testing

A driver who successfully completes the return-to-duty process and returns to safety-sensitive duties shall be subject to unannounced follow-up testing as directed by the SAP.

The number and frequency of follow-up tests will be determined by the SAP in accordance with 49 CFR Part 40.

Follow-up testing is separate from and in addition to random testing.

Follow-up testing will be conducted under direct observation as required by DOT regulations.

## 8. ALCOHOL RESULTS BETWEEN 0.02 AND 0.039

A driver with an alcohol concentration of **0.02 through 0.039** shall not perform or continue to perform safety-sensitive functions.

The driver shall not return to safety-sensitive functions until the start of the driver's next regularly scheduled duty period, but not less than **24 hours following administration of the test**.

An alcohol result of 0.02 through 0.039 is not, by itself, the same as a DOT alcohol violation of 0.04 or greater; however, federal regulations prohibit the driver from performing safety-sensitive functions during the required removal period.

Any additional employment consequences imposed by {company} beyond DOT requirements must be imposed under the Company's independent authority and identified as such.

---

## 9. REFUSAL TO TEST

A refusal to test constitutes a DOT drug and alcohol program violation.

Conduct that may constitute a refusal includes, but is not limited to:

- Failing to appear for a required test within the required or reasonable time;
- Failing to remain at the testing site until the testing process is complete;
- Failing to provide a required specimen;
- Failing to provide a sufficient specimen without an adequate medical explanation as determined under DOT procedures;
- Failing to cooperate with any part of the testing process;
- Failing to permit a directly observed or monitored collection when required;
- Failing to undergo a required medical examination or evaluation;
- Admitting to adulterating or substituting a specimen;
- Possessing or wearing a prosthetic or other device that could be used to interfere with the collection process;
- Failing to sign required portions of an alcohol testing form;
- Leaving the scene of an accident before required testing without a valid reason; or
- Otherwise engaging in conduct defined by 49 CFR Part 40 or Part 382 as a refusal.

The determination that conduct constitutes a refusal shall be made by the appropriate party as provided by DOT regulations.

---

## 10. CONSEQUENCES OF A DOT VIOLATION

A driver shall be immediately removed from DOT-regulated safety-sensitive functions upon receipt of information requiring removal under DOT regulations, including:

- A verified positive drug test;
- A verified adulterated or substituted specimen constituting a refusal;
- An alcohol confirmation test result of 0.04 or greater;
- A refusal to submit to a required DOT test;
- Actual knowledge of prohibited alcohol or controlled substance conduct; or
- Other conduct constituting a DOT drug or alcohol violation.

The driver shall not perform a DOT-regulated safety-sensitive function for {company} or any other DOT-regulated employer until the driver has successfully completed the applicable return-to-duty process.

The Company shall provide the driver with information concerning qualified Substance Abuse Professionals as required by 49 CFR Part 40.

## 11. SUBSTANCE ABUSE PROFESSIONAL AND RETURN-TO-DUTY PROCESS

When a driver commits a DOT drug or alcohol violation, {company} will provide the driver with a list of qualified Substance Abuse Professionals ("SAPs") as required by 49 CFR 40.287.

The driver must successfully complete the SAP's requirements before becoming eligible for return-to-duty testing.

The Company is not required by DOT regulations to retain or reinstate an employee merely because the employee completes the SAP process.

## 13. PRESCRIPTION AND OVER-THE-COUNTER MEDICATIONS

Drivers are responsible for ensuring that prescription medications, over-the-counter medications, and other substances they use do not impair their ability to safely perform safety-sensitive functions.

A driver shall not perform a safety-sensitive function while using a controlled substance unless its use is consistent with applicable federal requirements, including where applicable instructions from a licensed medical practitioner who has advised the driver that the substance will not adversely affect the driver's ability to safely operate a commercial motor vehicle.

Drivers should discuss the safety-sensitive nature of their duties with their treating medical practitioner.

Drivers are not required by this policy to disclose unrelated confidential medical information except where disclosure is required by law, DOT regulation, medical qualification requirements, or another lawful Company policy.

---

## 14. MARIJUANA, CANNABIS, CBD, AND STATE LAW

Marijuana remains prohibited for purposes of the federal DOT drug testing program.

A state law authorizing medical or recreational marijuana does not change the requirements of the federal DOT drug testing program.

A medical marijuana authorization or recommendation does not constitute a valid medical explanation for a positive DOT marijuana test.

Drivers are also cautioned that some CBD and hemp-derived products may contain THC. Use of such products does not excuse a positive DOT drug test.

Covered drivers are responsible for ensuring their compliance with federal DOT requirements.

## 15. DRIVER NOTIFICATION AND IMMEDIATE REPORTING

When a driver is notified of a required random, reasonable suspicion, post-accident, return-to-duty, or follow-up test, the driver must comply with the Company's instructions and proceed to the designated testing location without unnecessary delay.

A driver must immediately notify the DER or designated supervisor of circumstances that may trigger post-accident testing requirements.

Failure to remain available for required post-accident testing may constitute a refusal to test.

## 16. SUPERVISOR TRAINING

Each Company supervisor or official designated to determine whether reasonable suspicion exists to require testing shall receive at least:

- **60 minutes of training concerning alcohol misuse; and**
- **60 minutes of training concerning controlled substances use.**

The training will address physical, behavioral, speech, and performance indicators of probable alcohol misuse and controlled substances use.

Only appropriately trained supervisors or Company officials will make reasonable suspicion determinations required by Part 382.

## 19. DOT AND NON-DOT TESTING

DOT tests and non-DOT tests are separate testing processes.

When {company} conducts testing under its own authority in addition to DOT testing:

1. The employee will be informed whether the test is a DOT or non-DOT test;
2. DOT testing will be performed according to 49 CFR Part 40;
3. A DOT specimen will not be tested for additional non-DOT substances; and
4. Any non-DOT testing will be performed separately under the Company's independent authority.

Company rules exceeding DOT requirements shall not be represented as DOT requirements.
