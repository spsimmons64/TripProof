1. PURPOSE AND POLICY

{company} adopts this System Safety Program Plan ("SSPP") to establish and maintain a comprehensive safety program for its passenger transportation operations.

The objectives of this SSPP are to:

  • Protect passengers, employees, drivers, pedestrians, and the public;
  • Provide safe and reliable passenger transportation;
  • Establish clear responsibility for operational safety;
  • Establish minimum qualifications for drivers and other safety-sensitive employees;
  • Establish vehicle inspection and maintenance requirements;
  • Establish safe driving and passenger-assistance procedures;
  • Identify, report, investigate, and correct safety hazards;
  • Establish procedures for responding to accidents, incidents, emergencies, and unsafe conditions;
  • Provide and document employee safety training;
  • Maintain required safety records; and
  • Comply with applicable federal, state, local, contractual, and program requirements.

The Company will implement and operate in accordance with this SSPP whenever the Plan applies to its transportation operations.

No employee, supervisor, dispatcher, manager, contractor, or other person acting on behalf of the Company may knowingly direct or require another person to violate an applicable safety requirement.

When a conflict exists between this Plan and an applicable law or regulation, the applicable law or regulation controls.

2. REGULATORY AUTHORITY

This SSPP is intended to address applicable requirements of:

  • Chapter 341, Florida Statutes;
  • Rule Chapter 14-90, Florida Administrative Code;
  • Applicable Florida Department of Transportation requirements;
  • Applicable Florida motor vehicle and driver licensing laws;
  • Applicable Federal Motor Carrier Safety Regulations when operations, vehicles, or drivers fall within their scope;
  • Applicable Americans with Disabilities Act requirements;
  • Applicable drug and alcohol testing requirements;
  • Applicable Medicaid/NEMT program requirements;
  • Applicable broker and transportation-network contracts; and
  • Other federal, state, or local safety requirements applicable to the Company's operations.

Compliance obligations shall be determined according to the Company's actual operations, vehicles, contracts, funding sources, and regulatory status.

A contractual or Company requirement may exceed a regulatory minimum but shall not be represented as a legal requirement unless imposed by law or regulation.

3. SCOPE

This SSPP applies to Company transportation operations that are subject to the Plan.

It applies, as appropriate, to:

  • Company officers and management;
  • Safety personnel;
  • Drivers;
  • CDL/CMV drivers;
  • Non-CDL drivers;
  • Dispatchers;
  • Vehicle maintenance personnel;
  • Passenger-assistance personnel;
  • Supervisors;
  • Contractors performing covered transportation services; and
  • Other employees whose work affects transportation safety.

The Company shall determine and document the applicability of federal, state, contractual, broker, and Company-specific requirements to each operation and employee classification.

4. MANAGEMENT COMMITMENT TO SAFETY

Safety is a fundamental operating responsibility of {company}.

Management shall provide the authority, personnel, equipment, training, procedures, and resources reasonably necessary to operate safely and comply with applicable requirements.

Safety takes priority over:

  • Maintaining schedules;
  • Completing a trip;
  • Avoiding a late pickup;
  • Productivity targets;
  • Customer complaints concerning reasonable safety delays; and
  • Financial considerations when continued operation would create an unsafe condition.

No driver will be disciplined solely for refusing to operate a vehicle that the driver reasonably and in good faith believes presents an immediate safety hazard, pending appropriate evaluation of the condition.

Company management is responsible for:

  • Adopting and supporting this SSPP;
  • Assigning responsibility for implementation;
  • Providing adequate resources for safety activities;
  • Ensuring identified safety deficiencies are addressed;
  • Reviewing significant accidents and safety trends;
  • Ensuring employees are appropriately qualified and trained;
  • Ensuring vehicles are appropriately maintained; and
  • Ensuring required records are maintained.

5.3 Supervisors and Dispatchers

Supervisors and dispatchers shall:

  • Assign only qualified and authorized drivers;
  • Avoid assigning a driver known to be unfit for duty;
  • Avoid assigning a vehicle known to be unsafe;
  • Respond promptly to reported hazards;
  • Document significant safety events;
  • Escalate safety issues when necessary; and
  • Never pressure a driver to violate safety requirements.

5.4 Drivers

Drivers are responsible for:

  • Maintaining all required qualifications;
  • Maintaining a valid driver's license appropriate for the vehicle operated;
  • Reporting license suspensions, cancellations, revocations, restrictions, or other changes as required;
  • Reporting medical conditions or limitations when disclosure is legally required for safe operation;
  • Reporting for duty fit to safely operate a vehicle;
  • Conducting required vehicle inspections;
  • Reporting vehicle defects;
  • Following passenger-assistance and securement procedures;
  • Following safe-driving requirements;
  • Reporting accidents and incidents;
  • Reporting unsafe conditions;
  • Completing required training; and
  • Following this SSPP and other applicable Company safety procedures.

6. DRIVER SELECTION AND QUALIFICATION

The Company shall establish and maintain procedures to ensure that drivers meet all applicable qualification requirements before being permitted to operate Company vehicles without required supervision.

Qualification requirements will be based upon the type of vehicle operated and applicable federal, state, program, contractual, and Company requirements.

6.1 Driver Qualification Review

Before a driver is authorized to operate independently, the Company shall verify applicable requirements, which may include:

  • Completed employment/application information;
  • Identity verification;
  • Valid driver's license;
  • Correct license class;
  • Required endorsements;
  • Applicable license restrictions;
  • Motor vehicle record;
  • Criminal/background screening;
  • Required exclusion or sanction screening;
  • Required medical qualification;
  • Drug and alcohol testing requirements;
  • Prior-employer/safety-performance information when required;
  • Required Clearinghouse query for DOT-covered CDL drivers;
  • Required training;
  • Vehicle-specific competency;
  • Passenger-assistance competency;
  • Wheelchair/lift/securement competency when applicable; and
  • Other qualifications imposed by law, contract, broker, payer, or Company policy.

No driver shall be assigned to operate a vehicle for which the driver is not properly licensed, qualified, trained, and authorized.

7. DRIVER LICENSE MONITORING

The Company shall maintain documentation of required driver licensing.

A driver shall immediately report, or report within any shorter period required by applicable law, any:

  • Suspension;
  • Revocation;
  • Cancellation;
  • Disqualification;
  • Expiration;
  • Material restriction; or
  • Other action affecting the driver's legal authority to operate the assigned vehicle.

A driver whose license is invalid, expired, suspended, canceled, revoked, or otherwise insufficient for the assigned vehicle shall not operate that vehicle.

The Company shall conduct MVR reviews at the intervals required by applicable law, contract, program requirement, or Company policy.

8. MEDICAL QUALIFICATION AND FITNESS FOR DUTY

Drivers shall meet applicable medical qualification requirements.

Where Florida Rule Chapter 14-90 medical examination requirements apply, the Company shall maintain the required medical qualification documentation.

Where federal DOT medical qualification requirements apply, the driver shall also satisfy applicable federal requirements.

The Company shall not knowingly permit a driver to operate when the driver's ability to safely drive is impaired or likely to be impaired by:

  • Illness;
  • Injury;
  • Fatigue;
  • Medication;
  • Alcohol;
  • Controlled substances;
  • Emotional or physical condition; or
  • Another condition that makes operation unsafe.

Drivers must immediately notify dispatch or management when they become unable to safely continue driving.

9. DRUG AND ALCOHOL SAFETY

The Company shall maintain applicable drug and alcohol policies.

Drivers subject to FMCSA drug and alcohol regulations shall comply with the Company's DOT/CDL Drug and Alcohol Testing Policy and applicable federal requirements.

Other employees shall comply with the Company's non-DOT Drug and Alcohol Workplace Policy where applicable.

No driver may operate a Company vehicle while impaired.

DOT testing and non-DOT testing shall be administered separately as required.

10. DRIVER TRAINING PROGRAM

No driver shall operate independently until completing required initial training and demonstrating competency appropriate to the vehicle and duties assigned.

Training shall include, as applicable:

10.1 Company Safety and Operating Procedures

Drivers shall receive instruction concerning:

  • This SSPP;
  • Company safety policies;
  • Dispatch procedures;
  • Accident reporting;
  • Emergency procedures;
  • Vehicle-defect reporting;
  • Passenger safety;
  • Prohibited conduct; and
  • Safety-reporting responsibilities.

10.2 Vehicle Familiarization

Drivers shall receive instruction and demonstrate competency in:

  • Starting and shutting down the vehicle;
  • Vehicle controls;
  • Mirrors;
  • Braking;
  • Turning;
  • Backing;
  • Parking;
  • Emergency equipment;
  • Doors;
  • Ramps;
  • Lifts;
  • Securement equipment; and
  • Other vehicle-specific systems.

10.3 Vehicle Inspection

Drivers shall be trained to perform required inspections and identify safety-related defects.

10.4 Passenger Boarding and Alighting

Training shall include procedures for:

  • Approaching pickup/drop-off locations;
  • Selecting safe stopping locations;
  • Pedestrian awareness;
  • Passenger boarding;
  • Passenger exiting;
  • Door operation;
  • Mobility assistance;
  • Preventing falls; and
  • Safely departing the stop.

10.5 Wheelchair and Mobility Device Transportation

Drivers transporting passengers using mobility devices shall receive training in:

  • Lift/ramp operation;
  • Wheelchair positioning;
  • Wheelchair securement;
  • Occupant restraints;
  • Inspection of securement equipment;
  • Passenger communication;
  • Safe assistance techniques; and
  • Emergency procedures involving mobility-device passengers.

10.6 Defensive Driving

Training shall address:

  • Following distance;
  • Speed management;
  • Intersection safety;
  • Pedestrian and cyclist awareness;
  • Adverse weather;
  • Night driving;
  • Space management;
  • Hazard recognition;
  • Distracted driving; and
  • Preventable accident reduction.

10.7 Emergency and Security Awareness

Drivers shall receive training concerning:

  • Vehicle accidents;
  • Passenger medical emergencies;
  • Vehicle fires;
  • Evacuations;
  • Disabled vehicles;
  • Threats or violence;
  • Suspicious activity;
  • Emergency communications; and
  • Contact with law enforcement and emergency responders.

10.8 Wireless Communication Devices

Drivers shall receive training concerning:

  • Hazards associated with distracted driving;
  • Company restrictions on wireless devices;
  • Proper use of Company-issued communications equipment; and
  • Procedures for safely stopping before using a device when required.

Training completion shall be documented.

11. VEHICLE AND EQUIPMENT SAFETY

Vehicles used in covered operations shall meet applicable safety and equipment standards.

The Company shall establish procedures for:

  • Vehicle procurement;
  • Vehicle acceptance;
  • Required safety equipment;
  • Accessibility equipment;
  • Wheelchair lifts and ramps;
  • Wheelchair securement equipment;
  • Emergency equipment;
  • Preventive maintenance;
  • Vehicle inspections;
  • Defect reporting; and
  • Removal of unsafe vehicles from service.

A vehicle shall not be dispatched when a known defect makes operation unsafe or violates an applicable out-of-service requirement.

12. PRE-TRIP AND POST-TRIP INSPECTIONS

Drivers shall conduct required vehicle inspections according to Company procedures and applicable requirements.

12.1 Pre-Trip Inspection

Before placing a vehicle into service, the driver shall inspect applicable items, including:

  • Tires and wheels;
  • Lights;
  • Mirrors;
  • Windshield and wipers;
  • Horn;
  • Brakes or observable brake indicators;
  • Steering;
  • Doors;
  • Seat belts;
  • Emergency equipment;
  • Fluid leaks;
  • Warning indicators;
  • Wheelchair lift/ramp;
  • Wheelchair securement equipment;
  • Interior passenger area; and
  • Other required safety equipment.

12.2 Post-Trip Inspection

At the conclusion of service, the driver shall:

  • Check the passenger compartment;
  • Ensure no passenger remains aboard;
  • Check for lost property;
  • Identify observable damage;
  • Identify safety defects;
  • Report defects; and
  • Complete required inspection documentation.

Safety-critical defects shall be reported immediately.

13. VEHICLE DEFECTS AND OUT-OF-SERVICE PROCEDURES

Drivers shall not knowingly operate a vehicle with a defect that makes continued operation unsafe.

When a safety defect is discovered:

  • The driver shall notify dispatch or management.
  • The defect shall be documented.
  • The Company shall determine whether the vehicle may safely remain in service.
  • Vehicles determined unsafe shall be removed from service.
  • Necessary repairs shall be completed.
  • Repair completion shall be documented.
  • The vehicle shall not return to service until it is safe to operate.

Drivers shall not be directed to ignore or conceal safety defects.

14. PREVENTIVE MAINTENANCE

The Company shall maintain a preventive maintenance program appropriate to each vehicle.

Maintenance schedules shall consider:

  • Manufacturer recommendations;
  • Mileage;
  • Time intervals;
  • Vehicle duty cycle;
  • Applicable regulatory requirements;
  • Inspection findings; and
  • Repair history.

Maintenance records shall identify, as appropriate:

  • Vehicle;
  • Date;
  • Mileage;
  • Inspection or maintenance performed;
  • Defects identified;
  • Repairs completed; and
  • Person or vendor performing the work.

15. PERIODIC/ANNUAL VEHICLE SAFETY INSPECTIONS

Covered vehicles shall receive safety inspections at the frequency required by applicable law and program requirements.

When Rule Chapter 14-90 applies, operable transit buses shall receive the required annual safety inspection.

Inspection deficiencies shall be corrected as required before continued operation.

Inspection documentation shall be maintained by the Company.

16. SAFE DRIVING REQUIREMENTS

Drivers shall operate vehicles safely and lawfully at all times.

Drivers shall:

  • Obey traffic laws;
  • Operate at a safe speed;
  • Maintain appropriate following distance;
  • Use seat belts;
  • Ensure passengers use required restraints;
  • Avoid aggressive driving;
  • Exercise additional caution around pedestrians;
  • Use mirrors appropriately;
  • Use appropriate precautions when backing;
  • Adjust operation for weather and road conditions;
  • Avoid unsafe distractions;
  • Follow railroad-crossing requirements where applicable; and
  • Follow Company procedures concerning pickup and drop-off locations.

No schedule or dispatch instruction authorizes unsafe operation.

17. WIRELESS COMMUNICATION DEVICES

Drivers shall not use wireless communication devices in a manner prohibited by law or Company policy while operating a vehicle.

Texting, reading messages, entering information, browsing, or other distracting device use while driving is prohibited.

Company-issued dispatch or navigation equipment shall be used only in a safe and lawful manner.

When interaction with a device cannot be performed safely and legally while the vehicle is moving, the driver shall stop at a safe location before using the device.

Emergency communications shall be handled consistent with applicable law and immediate safety needs.

18. PASSENGER SAFETY AND ASSISTANCE

Drivers shall provide reasonable and safe assistance consistent with their training and the passenger's needs.

Drivers shall:

  • Stop in a reasonably safe location;
  • Secure the vehicle before boarding or alighting passengers;
  • Provide required passenger assistance;
  • Use available accessibility equipment properly;
  • Ensure required passenger restraints are used;
  • Secure mobility devices appropriately;
  • Avoid unnecessary physical handling of passengers;
  • Protect passenger dignity; and
  • Report passenger injuries or safety incidents.

Drivers shall not provide medical treatment outside the driver's training and authorized scope.

19. WHEELCHAIR AND MOBILITY DEVICE SAFETY

Before moving the vehicle, the driver shall verify that applicable wheelchair and occupant securement procedures have been completed.

Securement equipment shall be inspected for obvious defects.

Defective securement equipment shall be reported.

Drivers shall not improvise unsafe securement methods.

Passengers using mobility devices shall be treated respectfully and provided assistance consistent with applicable accessibility requirements and Company procedures.

20. FATIGUE AND HOURS OF SERVICE

No driver shall operate when fatigue makes operation unsafe.

Drivers shall report fatigue that prevents safe operation.

The Company shall schedule work consistent with applicable federal or state hours-of-service limitations.

Dispatchers and supervisors shall not knowingly require a driver to exceed applicable driving or duty limitations.

Where federal FMCSA hours-of-service regulations apply, those requirements shall control.

21. ACCIDENT AND INCIDENT RESPONSE

Drivers shall immediately report accidents and serious safety incidents.

Following an accident, the driver's priorities are:

  • Stop safely.
  • Protect passengers.
  • Call emergency services when necessary.
  • Provide reasonable assistance within the driver's training.
  • Notify dispatch or management.
  • Protect the scene when safely possible.
  • Cooperate with law enforcement.
  • Obtain required information.
  • Avoid unnecessary admissions or speculation concerning fault.
  • Complete Company accident documentation.
  • Remain available for required testing when applicable.

DOT-covered drivers shall follow applicable DOT post-accident drug and alcohol testing procedures.

22. ACCIDENT AND SAFETY EVENT INVESTIGATION

The Company shall investigate safety events requiring investigation under applicable regulations or Company policy.

The purpose of the investigation is to determine what occurred, identify contributing factors, identify hazards, and prevent recurrence.

Investigations may include:

  • Driver statement;
  • Passenger/witness statements;
  • Police report;
  • Photographs;
  • Vehicle condition;
  • Inspection and maintenance records;
  • Driver qualification records;
  • Training records;
  • Dispatch records;
  • GPS/telematics information;
  • Video where available and lawfully used;
  • Weather and road conditions;
  • Drug/alcohol testing information when legally available;
  • Applicable policies and procedures; and
  • Other relevant evidence.

Investigations shall focus on causal and contributing factors, not merely assigning blame.

23. CORRECTIVE ACTION

When an investigation, inspection, audit, complaint, or hazard report identifies a safety deficiency, the Company shall determine appropriate corrective action.

Corrective actions may include:

  • Vehicle repair;
  • Vehicle removal from service;
  • Employee retraining;
  • Procedure revision;
  • Increased supervision;
  • Route or operational changes;
  • Equipment changes;
  • Disciplinary action where appropriate;
  • Vendor corrective action;
  • Additional monitoring; or
  • Other measures necessary to address the hazard.

Corrective actions shall identify the issue, the person responsible, the action required, the target completion date, the completion date, and the method of verification. Appendix E is the form this Company uses to record them.

24. HAZARD IDENTIFICATION AND RESOLUTION

Employees are required and encouraged to report safety hazards.

Hazards may be identified through:

  • Driver reports;
  • Vehicle inspections;
  • Maintenance records;
  • Passenger complaints;
  • Accident investigations;
  • Near-miss reports;
  • Supervisor observations;
  • Internal audits;
  • Regulatory inspections;
  • Safety data analysis; and
  • Employee reports.

Reported hazards shall be evaluated based on:

  • Likelihood of occurrence;
  • Potential severity;
  • Exposure;
  • Ability to detect or prevent the condition; and
  • Need for immediate action.

An imminent hazard shall receive immediate attention.

The Company shall track significant hazards until resolved.

25. NEAR-MISS REPORTING

Employees are encouraged to report events that could reasonably have resulted in an accident, injury, or property damage but did not.

Near-miss reporting is intended to identify hazards before an injury or accident occurs.

Good-faith reporting of a safety concern or near miss will not, by itself, result in retaliation.

Intentional misconduct or knowingly unsafe behavior may still result in appropriate corrective or disciplinary action.

26. SAFETY DATA COLLECTION AND ANALYSIS

The Company shall collect and periodically review safety information.

Data may include:

  • Vehicle accidents;
  • Passenger injuries;
  • Employee injuries;
  • Preventable accidents;
  • Moving violations;
  • Driver complaints;
  • Passenger safety complaints;
  • Road calls;
  • Vehicle defects;
  • Maintenance failures;
  • Wheelchair/lift incidents;
  • Near misses;
  • Training deficiencies; and
  • Identified hazards.

Management shall review trends and determine whether additional corrective action is necessary.

27. EMERGENCY PROCEDURES

The Company shall maintain procedures for foreseeable emergencies, including:

  • Vehicle accidents;
  • Vehicle fires;
  • Passenger medical emergencies;
  • Vehicle breakdowns;
  • Severe weather;
  • Road closures;
  • Evacuations;
  • Threats or violence;
  • Missing or vulnerable passengers;
  • Communication failures; and
  • Other significant events.

Drivers shall know how to contact:

  • 911;
  • Company dispatch;
  • Company management; and
  • Other designated emergency contacts.

Passenger safety takes priority during an emergency.

28. VEHICLE BREAKDOWN PROCEDURE

When a vehicle becomes disabled:

  • Move the vehicle to a safe location when possible.
  • Activate warning devices as appropriate.
  • Protect passengers.
  • Notify dispatch.
  • Request emergency assistance when necessary.
  • Do not permit passengers to remain in an unsafe location.
  • Arrange replacement transportation when necessary.
  • Document the mechanical condition.
  • Do not resume operation until the vehicle can safely operate.

APPENDIX D - SAFETY EVENT CLASSIFICATION

The Company shall classify safety events for tracking and investigation purposes, which may include:

Accident: Vehicle collision or other event resulting in injury, fatality, or property damage.

Passenger Incident: Event involving a passenger injury, fall, boarding/alighting event, securement issue, or other passenger safety concern.

Vehicle Safety Event: Mechanical or equipment failure affecting safe operation.

Near Miss: Event that did not result in injury or damage but had reasonable potential to do so.

Hazard: Existing condition that could reasonably contribute to an accident, injury, or unsafe operation.

Classification under this Appendix is for Company safety management and does not replace any regulatory definition applicable to reporting or investigation.
