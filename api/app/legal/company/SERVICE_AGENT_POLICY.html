1. PURPOSE

This policy establishes procedures for administering federally regulated drug and alcohol testing and overseeing third-party service agents engaged by the Company.

The Company is committed to ensuring that all DOT-required testing is conducted in accordance with applicable federal regulations and that testing information is properly received, maintained, protected, and acted upon.

2. APPLICABILITY

This policy applies to the Company's drivers and employees subject to FMCSA drug and alcohol testing requirements under 49 CFR Part 382.

It also governs the Company's relationships with contracted testing service agents, including consortium/third-party administrators (C/TPAs), collection sites, laboratories, Medical Review Officers (MROs), alcohol-testing personnel, and Substance Abuse Professionals (SAPs).

This policy does not automatically apply to non-CDL NEMT drivers who are not subject to Part 382. Any non-DOT testing program shall be administered separately under applicable laws and Company policies.

3. EMPLOYER RESPONSIBILITY

The Company retains responsibility for compliance with applicable DOT drug and alcohol testing requirements, even when testing functions are performed by third-party service agents.

The Company shall:

  • Establish and maintain a compliant DOT testing program.
  • Ensure covered drivers are included in required testing.
  • Engage service agents capable of performing their assigned functions in compliance with DOT regulations.
  • Designate an employer representative responsible for receiving testing information and taking required actions.
  • Maintain required testing records and protect their confidentiality.
  • Promptly remove drivers from safety-sensitive functions when required by federal regulations.
  • Cooperate with authorized regulatory audits and investigations.

Delegation of testing administration does not transfer the Company's regulatory responsibilities to the service agent.

4. DESIGNATED EMPLOYER REPRESENTATIVE (DER)

The Company shall designate an employee to serve as its Designated Employer Representative.

The DER is authorized to receive testing results and other required communications, direct immediate removal of drivers from safety-sensitive functions, and coordinate required employer actions.

5. SELECTION AND OVERSIGHT OF SERVICE AGENTS

The Company may engage qualified third-party service agents to administer authorized portions of its DOT testing program.

Before engaging a service agent, the Company shall review the services provided and obtain appropriate documentation concerning the agent's qualifications, responsibilities, procedures, and communication arrangements.

The Company shall maintain records identifying its contracted service agents and the services each is authorized to perform.

Service agents must perform their functions in accordance with applicable requirements of 49 CFR Part 40.

6. CONSORTIUM/THIRD-PARTY ADMINISTRATOR (C/TPA)

The Company may use a C/TPA to coordinate testing activities, administer random selection pools, schedule testing, maintain authorized records, and perform other functions permitted under DOT regulations.

The Company shall establish procedures with its C/TPA for:

  • Adding and removing covered drivers from the testing program.
  • Maintaining accurate random testing pool information.
  • Communicating testing selections and instructions.
  • Receiving authorized testing information.
  • Reporting testing problems or incomplete testing events.
  • Maintaining records and obtaining information needed for regulatory reporting.
  • Transferring records when changing service providers.

The Company remains responsible for ensuring that required testing is completed and that appropriate actions are taken.

7. DOT TESTING PROCEDURES

All DOT-required controlled substances and alcohol testing shall be conducted using the procedures prescribed by 49 CFR Part 40.

Testing shall be performed by personnel and facilities meeting applicable DOT requirements.

DOT testing shall be identified and administered separately from any non-DOT testing program.

The Company shall not substitute a non-DOT test for a required DOT test.

8. REQUIRED TESTING CATEGORIES

The Company's testing program shall address all testing categories applicable under 49 CFR Part 382, including:

  • Pre-employment controlled substances testing.
  • Random controlled substances and alcohol testing.
  • Post-accident testing when regulatory criteria are met.
  • Reasonable-suspicion testing.
  • Return-to-duty testing.
  • Follow-up testing.

Testing shall be conducted only under the applicable regulatory authority and procedures.

9. MEDICAL REVIEW OFFICER (MRO)

DOT controlled substances testing results shall be reviewed and verified by an appropriately qualified Medical Review Officer.

The Company shall rely on the MRO's verified determination rather than independently interpreting laboratory findings.

The DER shall receive verified results through communication channels authorized by DOT regulations.

The Company shall not request or retain unnecessary medical information beyond what is authorized or required.

10. ALCOHOL TESTING

DOT alcohol screening and confirmation testing shall be conducted by qualified personnel using approved equipment and procedures.

The Company shall ensure that alcohol test results are communicated to the DER as required and that appropriate action is taken when a driver is prohibited from performing safety-sensitive functions.

11. TRANSMISSION OF TESTING INFORMATION

Testing results and related information shall be transmitted only through methods and intermediaries permitted under 49 CFR Part 40.

A C/TPA may act as an intermediary only where permitted by applicable DOT regulations.

The Company shall ensure that use of an intermediary does not interfere with required reporting deadlines, confidentiality protections, or immediate employer action.

12. CONFIDENTIALITY AND ACCESS

Drug and alcohol testing information shall be treated as confidential.

Access shall be limited to authorized personnel with a legitimate regulatory or operational need.

Testing records shall be stored separately from general personnel information or protected through equivalent access controls.

Information shall be disclosed only as authorized or required by applicable regulations.

The Company shall establish procedures for securely transmitting, accessing, retaining, and disposing of testing records.

13. PROHIBITED SERVICE AGENT ACTIVITIES

The Company shall not authorize or permit service agents to perform activities prohibited by 49 CFR Part 40.

Service agents shall not make employment decisions reserved to the employer, improperly alter testing results, interfere with the independent responsibilities of MROs or SAPs, or disclose protected testing information without authorization.

The Company shall investigate and address known service agent compliance concerns.

14. POSITIVE TESTS, REFUSALS, AND OTHER VIOLATIONS

When the Company receives information establishing a DOT drug or alcohol testing violation, the DER shall ensure that the driver is immediately removed from applicable safety-sensitive functions as required.

The Company shall follow applicable FMCSA Clearinghouse reporting and verification requirements.

A driver who has committed a DOT violation shall not return to safety-sensitive functions unless all applicable return-to-duty requirements have been satisfied.

15. SUBSTANCE ABUSE PROFESSIONAL AND RETURN-TO-DUTY PROCEDURES

Where applicable, drivers shall be provided information concerning qualified Substance Abuse Professionals.

The Company shall ensure that any return-to-duty and follow-up testing is administered in accordance with the applicable SAP process and DOT regulations.

The Company shall not independently substitute alternative counseling, treatment, or testing arrangements for federally required procedures.

16. RECORDKEEPING AND RETENTION

The Company shall maintain drug and alcohol testing records for the periods required by 49 CFR §382.401 and applicable provisions of Part 40.

Records shall include, as applicable:

  • Testing program administration documentation.
  • Testing events and verified results.
  • Random testing selection and completion records.
  • Documentation of refusals and other violations.
  • Return-to-duty and follow-up testing documentation.
  • Required training and supervisor qualification records.
  • Service agent agreements and administrative records.
  • Annual testing summaries and MIS reports.

Records shall remain available for authorized regulatory review.

17. SERVICE AGENT CHANGES

When the Company changes a C/TPA, MRO, or other service provider, it shall coordinate the transfer or continued accessibility of required testing records.

The Company shall ensure that changes in providers do not interrupt mandatory testing, random pool participation, required notifications, or record retention.

18. MONITORING AND CORRECTIVE ACTION

The Company shall periodically review its testing administration procedures and service agent arrangements.

Identified deficiencies shall be documented and corrected.

Corrective action may include additional training, changes to administrative procedures, replacement of service agents, or other appropriate measures.

19. POLICY REVIEW

This policy shall be reviewed when applicable federal regulations change, when testing administration procedures materially change, or when the Company identifies a need for revision.

The Company shall maintain the effective version and a history of material revisions.
