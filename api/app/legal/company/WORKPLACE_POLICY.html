COMPANY DRUG AND ALCOHOL WORKPLACE POLICY

Company: {company}

Effective Date: {effectivedate}

Policy Administrator: {derline}

1. PURPOSE

{company} is committed to maintaining a safe, professional, and drug- and alcohol-free workplace.

Because the Company provides transportation services, including transportation of passengers who may be elderly, disabled, medically vulnerable, or otherwise dependent upon the safe conduct of Company personnel, employees are expected to report to work and perform their duties free from impairment.

This policy establishes the Company's rules regarding drugs, alcohol, intoxicating substances, impairment, testing, and fitness for duty.

This policy is adopted under the Company's independent authority except where an employee is specifically subject to U.S. Department of Transportation ("DOT") drug and alcohol testing regulations.

2. EMPLOYEES COVERED

This policy applies to all employees of {company}, including, as applicable:

  • Drivers;
  • Non-commercial drivers;
  • CDL/CMV drivers;
  • Dispatchers;
  • Supervisors and managers;
  • Administrative personnel;
  • Maintenance personnel;
  • Temporary employees; and
  • Other employees performing work on behalf of the Company.

Independent contractors may also be required to comply with applicable safety, impairment, and substance-use requirements as a condition of performing services for the Company.

DOT-Covered Employees

Employees subject to DOT/FMCSA drug and alcohol testing requirements under 49 CFR Parts 40 and 382 are additionally subject to the Company's CDL / CMV Drug and Alcohol Testing Policy.

When DOT regulations apply, the DOT-required testing process will be conducted separately from testing performed under this Company policy.

Nothing in this policy modifies, replaces, or reduces any obligation imposed by DOT regulations.

3. DRUG- AND ALCOHOL-FREE WORKPLACE

Employees are prohibited from reporting to work, remaining at work, driving, transporting passengers, operating Company vehicles or equipment, or otherwise performing Company duties while impaired by alcohol, illegal drugs, controlled substances, intoxicating substances, or medication that makes the employee unable to safely perform their duties.

Employees must be fit for duty whenever performing work for the Company.

4. PROHIBITED CONDUCT

Employees may not:

  • Use, possess, manufacture, distribute, sell, transfer, or dispense illegal drugs or unauthorized controlled substances while working or on Company property;
  • Consume or possess alcohol while working, driving, transporting passengers, or performing safety-sensitive duties unless specifically authorized for a legitimate Company purpose that does not involve operating a vehicle or performing safety-sensitive work;
  • Report to work or remain on duty while impaired by alcohol, drugs, controlled substances, intoxicating substances, or medication;
  • Operate a Company vehicle or transport a passenger while impaired;
  • Misuse prescription medication;
  • Use medication prescribed to another person;
  • Possess drug paraphernalia in a Company vehicle or workplace, except where possession is lawful and legitimately required for medical purposes;
  • Refuse a drug or alcohol test required under this policy;
  • Attempt to adulterate, substitute, dilute, manipulate, or otherwise interfere with a required test;
  • Fail to cooperate with a testing procedure or investigation required under this policy; or
  • Engage in conduct that creates an unreasonable safety risk because of drug or alcohol use.

5. SAFETY-SENSITIVE EMPLOYEES

For purposes of this Company policy, a safety-sensitive employee includes an employee whose duties could directly affect the safety of passengers, patients, coworkers, or the public.

This includes employees who:

  • Operate passenger transportation vehicles;
  • Transport NEMT passengers;
  • Assist passengers into or out of vehicles;
  • Secure wheelchairs or other mobility devices;
  • Operate lifts or ramps;
  • Perform vehicle safety inspections;
  • Maintain or repair transportation vehicles;
  • Dispatch or direct drivers when impairment could create a safety risk; or
  • Perform other duties designated by the Company as safety-sensitive.

Safety-sensitive employees are held to heightened fitness-for-duty requirements because impairment may directly affect passenger and public safety.

6. PRESCRIPTION AND OVER-THE-COUNTER MEDICATION

The lawful use of prescription and over-the-counter medication is not prohibited merely because an employee uses medication.

However, employees may not perform duties when a medication causes impairment or creates an unreasonable safety risk.

An employee performing safety-sensitive duties is responsible for determining, in consultation with an appropriate healthcare provider when necessary, whether medication may impair the employee's ability to safely perform those duties.

The Company may require appropriate fitness-for-duty documentation when permitted by law and reasonably necessary to determine whether an employee can safely perform essential job functions.

Employees should not be required to disclose unrelated diagnoses or medical information beyond what is reasonably necessary and legally permitted.

7. MARIJUANA, CANNABIS, THC, CBD, AND OTHER INTOXICATING PRODUCTS

Employees may not report to work or perform Company duties while impaired by marijuana, cannabis, THC, or another intoxicating substance.

State authorization for medical or recreational marijuana does not permit an employee to work while impaired.

Employees performing DOT-regulated duties are subject to federal DOT requirements concerning marijuana regardless of state marijuana laws.

Employees should also understand that some CBD, hemp, and similar products may contain THC and may result in a positive drug test.

DOT-covered employees remain responsible for compliance with federal DOT drug-testing requirements.

8. TESTING UNDER COMPANY AUTHORITY

To the extent permitted by applicable federal, state, and local law, {company} may require drug and/or alcohol testing under its independent authority in circumstances including:

A. Pre-Employment Testing

The Company may require applicants for designated positions to successfully complete drug testing before beginning employment or safety-sensitive duties.

B. Reasonable Suspicion Testing

An employee may be required to undergo drug and/or alcohol testing when the Company has a reasonable basis to believe the employee may be impaired or may have violated this policy.

Indicators may include, but are not limited to:

  • Odor of alcohol or drugs;
  • Slurred speech;
  • Unsteady movement;
  • Unusual behavior;
  • Significant unexplained changes in behavior or performance;
  • Confusion or disorientation;
  • Observable evidence of substance use;
  • Possession of prohibited substances;
  • Credible information concerning prohibited substance use; or
  • Other specific observations indicating possible impairment.

Whenever practicable, reasonable-suspicion observations should be documented.

C. Post-Accident Testing

The Company may require drug and/or alcohol testing following a vehicle accident, passenger injury, workplace injury, or other safety-related incident when permitted by applicable law and Company policy.

Testing will not automatically be required merely because an accident or injury occurred when such automatic testing would be prohibited by applicable law.

D. Return-to-Duty/Fitness-for-Duty Testing

When permitted by law, the Company may require testing before allowing an employee to return to safety-sensitive duties following a violation of this policy.

E. Follow-Up Testing

Where legally permitted and appropriate under a return-to-work agreement, rehabilitation agreement, or other Company program, an employee may be subject to follow-up testing.

F. Random Testing

The Company may conduct random testing for employees or positions when authorized by applicable law, contract, funding source, regulatory requirement, or Company policy.

Because state laws concerning non-DOT workplace testing vary, the Company will conduct non-DOT random testing only when legally permissible.

9. DOT TESTING IS SEPARATE

Testing conducted under this Company policy is non-DOT testing unless the employee is specifically notified that the test is being conducted pursuant to DOT regulations.

DOT tests will be conducted according to applicable DOT regulations and the Company's separate CDL / CMV Drug and Alcohol Testing Policy.

DOT specimens will not be used to conduct additional non-DOT testing.

Company testing requirements shall not be represented to employees as DOT requirements when they are imposed solely under the Company's independent authority.

10. REFUSAL TO TEST

When testing is lawfully required under this policy, conduct considered a refusal may include:

  • Failing to report for testing as directed;
  • Leaving the testing location before completing the testing process;
  • Refusing to provide the required specimen;
  • Failing to cooperate with the collection or testing process;
  • Attempting to adulterate, substitute, or manipulate a specimen;
  • Providing an adulterated or substituted specimen;
  • Interfering with the testing process; or
  • Otherwise refusing to complete a properly authorized test.

A refusal to test may result in removal from safety-sensitive duties and disciplinary action, up to and including termination, subject to applicable law.

DOT refusals are governed separately by applicable DOT regulations.

11. POSITIVE TESTS AND POLICY VIOLATIONS

When an employee has a confirmed positive non-DOT drug or alcohol test or otherwise violates this policy, the Company may take appropriate action consistent with applicable law.

Depending upon the circumstances, such action may include:

  • Immediate removal from driving or other safety-sensitive duties;
  • Suspension;
  • Referral for evaluation or treatment;
  • A return-to-work agreement;
  • Additional testing;
  • Reassignment where appropriate;
  • Disciplinary action; or
  • Termination of employment.

The Company will consider applicable federal and state employment laws before taking employment action.

For DOT-covered employees, any DOT violation will additionally be handled according to the Company's CDL / CMV Drug and Alcohol Testing Policy and applicable federal regulations.

12. IMMEDIATE REMOVAL FROM DRIVING

Regardless of whether testing has been completed, the Company may immediately remove an employee from driving or other safety-sensitive duties when the Company reasonably believes the employee is impaired or otherwise unable to safely perform those duties.

A supervisor should arrange safe transportation when an employee suspected of impairment is removed from duty.

An employee suspected of impairment shall not be instructed or permitted to drive themselves from the workplace when doing so would create a safety risk.

13. ACCIDENTS AND INCIDENTS

Employees must immediately report vehicle accidents, passenger injuries, workplace injuries, or other serious safety incidents to their supervisor or designated Company representative.

Employees must cooperate with any investigation and testing required by applicable law or Company policy.

DOT-covered drivers must additionally comply with all DOT post-accident testing requirements.

Necessary emergency medical treatment shall take priority over Company testing procedures.

14. EMPLOYEE SELF-REPORTING AND REQUESTS FOR ASSISTANCE

Employees are encouraged to voluntarily seek assistance for substance-use problems before the problem results in a safety incident, policy violation, or required test.

An employee who voluntarily requests assistance before violating this policy may be eligible for leave, treatment, an employee assistance program, or other assistance when available and required or permitted by law.

A request for assistance does not excuse:

  • A violation that has already occurred;
  • A positive or refused test;
  • Reporting to work impaired;
  • Unsafe conduct; or
  • Failure to comply with DOT requirements.

The Company will address requests for assistance consistent with applicable law and available Company programs.

15. CONFIDENTIALITY

Drug and alcohol testing information and related medical information will be maintained confidentially to the extent required by law.

Information will be disclosed only to persons with a legitimate business or legal need for the information or when disclosure is authorized or required by law.

DOT drug and alcohol records will be maintained separately and according to applicable DOT recordkeeping and confidentiality requirements.

16. SEARCHES AND COMPANY PROPERTY

When permitted by applicable law, the Company reserves the right to inspect Company-owned vehicles, equipment, desks, lockers, containers, electronic equipment, and other Company property when reasonably necessary to investigate suspected violations of this policy.

Employees should not have an expectation of privacy in Company property except to the extent provided by applicable law or Company policy.

Any search involving an employee's personal property will be conducted only when permitted by applicable law and Company policy.

17. OFF-DUTY CONDUCT

The Company generally does not regulate lawful off-duty conduct unless the conduct affects the employee's ability to safely or lawfully perform their job, violates an applicable law or contractual requirement, creates a legitimate workplace safety concern, or otherwise provides a lawful basis for Company action.

Regardless of off-duty legality, no employee may report for work or perform safety-sensitive duties while impaired.

DOT-covered employees remain subject to applicable federal DOT requirements.

18. NON-RETALIATION

The Company prohibits retaliation against an employee for:

  • Reporting a legitimate safety concern;
  • Reporting suspected impairment in good faith;
  • Participating in a workplace investigation;
  • Requesting legally protected medical leave or accommodation; or
  • Exercising another right protected by applicable law.

Knowingly making a false report is not protected by this provision.

19. COMPLIANCE WITH STATE AND LOCAL LAW

Workplace drug and alcohol laws vary by jurisdiction.

{company} will administer this policy in accordance with applicable federal, state, and local law.

Where applicable law provides employees with greater protection or imposes additional testing procedures, notice requirements, laboratory requirements, medical review requirements, or other restrictions, the applicable law will control.

20. NO GUARANTEE OF EMPLOYMENT

Nothing in this policy creates a contract of employment or guarantees employment for any specific period.

The Company's ability to discipline or terminate an employee remains subject to applicable law, employment agreements, and Company policies.
