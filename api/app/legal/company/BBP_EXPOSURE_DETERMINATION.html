1. PURPOSE

This assessment identifies the job classifications, tasks and procedures within the Company that involve reasonably anticipated occupational exposure to blood or other potentially infectious materials (OPIM).

The determination is made in accordance with 29 CFR 1910.1030(c)(2), without regard to the use of personal protective equipment.

2. SCOPE OF THE ASSESSMENT

The Company has evaluated its transportation operations, employee responsibilities, passenger assistance activities, vehicle cleaning procedures and emergency response expectations to identify reasonably anticipated occupational exposure.

Occupational exposure means reasonably anticipated skin, eye, mucous membrane or parenteral contact with blood or OPIM arising from the performance of an employee's duties.

Incidental or unexpected contact that is not reasonably anticipated as part of assigned duties does not, by itself, establish occupational exposure.

4. JOB CLASSIFICATIONS IN WHICH SOME EMPLOYEES ARE EXPOSED

The classification, and the specific tasks or procedures that create the exposure.

{someexposed}

Activities in non-emergency medical transportation that call for evaluation include:

  • Assigned first aid involving anticipated contact with blood or OPIM.
  • Cleaning blood-contaminated vehicle surfaces or equipment.
  • Handling materials contaminated with blood or OPIM.
  • Assisting passengers with tasks involving reasonably anticipated blood or OPIM contact.
  • Handling contaminated sharps or regulated medical waste, where that is assigned.

An activity appearing on this list does not by itself establish exposure. The Company must evaluate whether the activity is actually part of the employee's assigned duties and whether occupational contact is reasonably anticipated.

5. JOB CLASSIFICATIONS WITH NO OCCUPATIONAL EXPOSURE IDENTIFIED

{noexposure}

A driver whose duties are limited to transportation and routine passenger assistance, and who is not assigned exposure-producing first aid or cleanup responsibilities, may be determined to have no occupational exposure once the actual duties have been evaluated.

6. HOW THE DETERMINATION WAS MADE

The Company confirms that this determination considered:

  • Actual employee job descriptions and assigned duties.
  • Passenger assistance responsibilities.
  • First aid and emergency response expectations.
  • Vehicle and equipment cleaning responsibilities.
  • Handling of potentially contaminated materials.
  • Relevant workplace procedures and operating conditions.
  • Applicable changes in job classifications or duties.

Personal protective equipment was not taken into account. 29 CFR 1910.1030(c)(2)(ii) requires the determination to be made without regard to whether employees wear gloves, masks, protective clothing or anything else: the question is what the work exposes somebody to, not what they are given to wear against it.

7. WHAT FOLLOWS FROM THIS DETERMINATION

Occupational exposure identified: {exposurefound}

Where occupational exposure is identified, the Company shall maintain a written Bloodborne Pathogens Exposure Control Plan and implement the applicable OSHA protections, addressing exposure controls, employee training, hepatitis B vaccination, post-exposure evaluation and follow-up, and recordkeeping.

Where no occupational exposure is identified, the Company has not identified employees covered by the occupational exposure provisions of 29 CFR 1910.1030. That conclusion is reassessed if duties or conditions change.

8. REVIEW AND REASSESSMENT

This determination is incorporated into the Company's Exposure Control Plan where such a plan is required.

The Exposure Control Plan is reviewed and updated at least annually, and whenever necessary to reflect new or modified tasks, procedures or employee positions affecting occupational exposure.

Where no occupational exposure is identified, the Company reassesses the determination when job duties or operating conditions materially change.
