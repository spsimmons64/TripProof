# A Summary of Your Rights Under the Fair Credit Reporting Act

The federal Fair Credit Reporting Act (FCRA) promotes the accuracy, fairness and privacy of
information held by consumer reporting agencies. In short, it gives you the right to:

- be told if information in your file has been used against you;
- know what is in your file, and to ask the agency for a copy;
- ask for your credit score, where one exists;
- dispute information you believe is incomplete or inaccurate, and have it investigated;
- have inaccurate, incomplete or unverifiable information corrected or deleted;
- have outdated negative information left out, in most cases after seven years;
- limit who may access your file, which requires a permissible purpose such as employment;
- give your consent before a report is given to an employer; and
- seek damages from anyone who violates the Act.

Identity theft victims and active duty military personnel have additional rights.
